The Ringed Planet

The robotic exploration of Saturn faces similar challenges to those of the missions to Jupiter. Saturn also represent a unique opportunity to study a large, complex ring system up close, and explore an atmosphere completely different compared to anything in the inner Solar System on the moon Titan.

Cassini

The Cassini mission to Saturn, the source of Figure \(\PageIndex{1}\), was a cooperative venture between NASA and the European Space Agency. It was similar to Galileo in its two-fold approach. Launched in 1997, Cassini arrived in 2004 and went into orbit around Saturn, beginning extensive studies of its rings and moons, as well as the planet itself. In January 2005, Cassini deployed an entry probe into the atmosphere of Saturn's large moon, Titan, where it successfully landed on the surface. We'll discuss the probe and what it found in the chapter on Rings, Moons, and Pluto.

Basic Characteristics

Let's review Table \(\PageIndex{1}\), with a focus on comparing Jupiter and Saturn. As mentioned previously, Jupiter is nearly four times more massive than Saturn. At the same time Jupiter is only about 20% larger than Saturn. This means that Saturn distributes a mass that is one quarter of Jupiter's over an area that is only slightly smaller. This leads to Saturn's low density of 0.7 g/cm3. One fact that many people remember about Saturn is that, if there was a large enough container, Saturn would float in water.
