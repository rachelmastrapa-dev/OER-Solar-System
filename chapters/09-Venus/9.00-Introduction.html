Introduction

Earth's sister planet is definitely not an identical twin. Earth and Venus are close in size, but that is where the similarity ends. From its rotation rate to surface temperature, Venus is an example of how small changes, such as distance to the Sun, can create large changes. If two planets that are close in size can be this different, imagine the many varieties of planets that may be out there in the universe. Let's explore Venus.

Learning Objectives
  • Explain why it's difficult to learn about Venus from Earth-based observation alone
  • Explain what the study of craters on Venus tells us about the age of its surface
  • Compare tectonic activity and volcanoes on Venus with those of Earth
  • Explain how the surface of Venus is inhospitable to human life
  • Describe the general composition and structure of the atmosphere on Venus
  • Explain how the greenhouse effect has led to high temperatures on Venus
