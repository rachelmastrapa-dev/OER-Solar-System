Introduction

We have already discussed the Moon and its phases. This chapter will discuss the Moon itself. We will review the formation of the Moon, and how its surface has changes over time. Unlike Earth, the Moon does not have active plates and volcanoes to change its surface. It also does not have water, a major driver of erosion on Earth. Impact cratering is the primary method of changing the Moon's surface. This process is important for other Solar System objects as well, so we will take a close look at how craters form.

Learning Objectives
  • Differentiate between the two major regions of the Moon
  • Describe the history of the lunar surface
  • Describe the properties of the lunar soil
  • Describe the process of impact crater formation
  • Describe the method of using crater counts to determine relative ages of regions on the lunar surface
  • Summarize the current giant impact concept of how the Moon formed
