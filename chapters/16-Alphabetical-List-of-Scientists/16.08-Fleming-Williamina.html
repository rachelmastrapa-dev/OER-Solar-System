Williamina Paton Stevens Fleming

Williamina Paton Stevens Fleming was born in Scotland in 1857 (Figure \(\PageIndex{1}\)). She and her husband, James Orr Fleming, moved to Boston, Massachusetts in 1878, where her husband abandoned her and their young son. She worked as a housekeeper for Harvard astronomer Edward Pickering. On the advice of his wife, Pickering hired Fleming to work part-time at the Harvard College Observatory, where she eventually became one of the Harvard Computers.

In the 1880s, Fleming devised a system to classify stars based on the strength of hydrogen absorption lines. Spectra with the strongest lines were classified as “A” stars, the next strongest “B,” and so on down the alphabet to “O” stars, in which the hydrogen lines were very weak. This classification system was used for more than 10,000 stars published in the Henry Draper Catalogue. Over her lifetime, she cataloged 52 nebulae, and discovered 310 variable stars. She is credited with the discovery of the Horsehead Nebula and the first white dwarf.
