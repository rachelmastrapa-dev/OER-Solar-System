Structure

Each chapter in this textbook begins with an introduction that summarizes the chapter and lists the chapter learning objectives. Each page in the chapter has contents related to the chapter topic. Chapters about a specific object, such as Mars, will be broken down into exploration, surface, atmosphere, and history of that object. Each page has been remixed from other open educational resources (OER). Links to any original sources in Libretext are automatically linked at the bottom of the page, below the main text. Any OER sources outside of Libretext, if used, are listed in a section at the bottom called attributions. Any references used for a page will also be listed at the end. Some pages will have examples and exercises or a box of links to external sources called Further Exploration.

Each chapter will have at least one page about science and society. These pages will draw a direct connection between astronomy and human life by sharing a story about history, a present day astronomer, the intersection of art and astronomy, and many other topics. Hopefully, these pages will help you make a more personal connection to astronomy and science in general.

Student Guide

This textbook is published in Libretext, a collection of over 3,000 free OER textbooks. You do not need an account to access the textbook. At any point while accessing the textbook, the button with a question mark (?) in the bottom right corner will take you to helpful information or a link to start a support ticket.

The main page for a textbook has tiles that represent each chapter. When on a page in the textbook, you can access different parts of the book through a contents menu in the upper right or on the left navigation bar. Also in the left navigation toolbar, there is a readability tool where you can adjust font and margin size. There is also a Beeline tool that you can use to vary the color or brightness of words in a sentence.

If you would like to save a version of the textbook for offline reading, you can save an individual page, or the entire textbook as a PDF by clicking on the red button labeled PDF above and to the right of the page text. There is a purple button labeled Buy Print Copy that will take you to an online store that will print the book at cost. Your school bookstore may also offer a print copy, so don't forget to price shop if you want a print version.

There is also a highlight, annotating, and commenting service called Hypothesis accessed by clicking on the arrow on the top right of the page. You will have to create an account, but you will be able to keep all of your notes and highlights for any textbook you use. You also have the option of keeping your notes private or sharing them with a group. You could use this to form a study group in your class and share notes and comments in the book.

In the upper right-hand corner, you can click on Enter Reader Mode for a more book-like appearance, clearing most of the other information off of the page. In this mode you can navigate pages with arrows on the right and left. Click Exit Reader Mode to leave.

Instructor Guide

This book is published under a Creative Commons Attribution license, so you are free to copy, disseminate, and adapt any part of this book as long as you attribute the source. Some of the examples of using this textbook below have built-in attribution mechanisms. If you choose to manually copy the text please include an attribution statement. Any attribution statement should include TASL: title, author, source, and license with links to each component. Please use something similar to:

This section was adapted from Mars in The Solar System by Rachel Mastrapa and Caitlin Kepple and published under a Creative Commons Attribution license 4.0.

Note that many of the figures in this text have their own authors and sources and should have the original attribution and be utilized according to their licenses.

To share this textbook with your students you can share the link to the main page of the textbook, or to individual chapters. You can also import textbooks directly into an LMS. You can also create your own version of the textbook in Libretext.

Rearrange This Textbook

To edit books in Libretext, you will need to create an account and apply for instructor approval. Once you have approval you can use multiple different tools provided by Libretext. The Conductor is used to edit textbooks.

One thing you can do is create a remix of this textbook. In this case, you do not intend to edit individual pages, but would like to reduce the number of chapters, pages, or change the order of the material in the textbook. You can use the Conductor to access the textbook in the Libretext collection and copy the material into your version one page or chapter at a time. Once it is copied you can change the order of individual pages. You can also find material from other sources and copy them into your book. Any material mixed through Conductor should automatically create an attribution statement with links at the bottom of each page.

Remix This Textbook

If you would like to make significant changes to the individual pages, then you can first follow the instructions for the remix to create your book. You will then need to fork any pages that you want to edit. The attribution statements will remain at the end of the page, but you will be the new author. Again, remember to manually attribute any OER that is not added through Conductor. The Further Exploration box has links to guides for remixing and editing OER in Libretext.
