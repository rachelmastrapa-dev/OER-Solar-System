Structure

Each chapter in this textbook begins with an introduction that summarizes the chapter and lists the chapter learning objectives. Each page in the chapter has contents related to the chapter topic. Chapters about a specific object, such as Mars, will be broken down into exploration, surface, atmosphere, and history of that object. Each page has been remixed from other open educational resources (OER). Links to some any original sources in Libretext are automatically linked at the bottom of the page, below the main text. Any OER sources outside of Libretext, if used, are listed in a section at the bottom called attributions. Any references used for a page will also be listed at the end. Some pages will have examples and exercises or a box of links to external sources called Further Exploration.

Each chapte will have at least one page about science and society. These pages will draw a direct connection between astronomy and human life by sharing a story about history, a present day astronomer, the intersection of art and astronomy, and many other topics. Hopefully, these pages will help you make a more personal connection astronomy and science in general.

Student Guide

This textbook is published in Libretext, a collection of over 3000 free, OER textbooks. You do not need an account to access the textbook. At any point while accessing the textbook, the button with a question mark (?) in the bottom right corner will take you to helpful information or a link to start a support ticket.

The main page for a textbook has tiles that represent each chapter. When on a page in the texbook, you can access different parts of the book through a contents menu in the upper right or on the left navigation bar. Also in the left navigation toolbar, there is a readability tool where you can adjust font and margin size. There is also a Beeline tool that you can use to vary the color or brightness of words in a sentence.

If you would like to save a version of the textbook for offline reading, you can save an individual page, or the entire textbook as a pdf by clicking on the red button labelled pdf above and to the right of the page text. There is a purple button labelled Buy Print Copy that will take you to an online store that will print the book at cost. You school bookstore may also offer a print copy, so don't forget to price shop if you want a print version.

There is also a highlight, annotating, and commenting service called Hypothesis accessed by clicking on the arrow on the top right of the page. You will have to create and account, but you will be able to keep all of your notes and highlights for any textbook you use. You also have the option of keeping your notes private or sharing them with a group. You could use this to form a study group in your class and share notes and comments in the book.

In the upper right hand corner, you can click on Enter Reader Mode for a more book like appearance, clearing most of the other information off of the page. In this mode you can navigate pages with arrows on the right and left. Click Exit Reader Mode to leave.
