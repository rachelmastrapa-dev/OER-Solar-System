Astronomy Toolkit Key Terms

  • absorption spectrum: The unique pattern of dark lines that form when light passes through a material and into a spectrometer
  • atomic number: The number of protons in an atom, .
  • blackbody: an idealized object that absorbs all electromagnetic energy that falls onto it
  • boiling point: the temperature where a liquid vaporizes into a gas; the temperature depends on pressure
  • centripetal acceleration: the acceleration of an object moving in uniform circular motion
  • condensation: conversion of a gas to a liquid
  • conduction: the transfer of energy between two objects that are in direct physical contact with each other or the transfer of energy inside an object from one side to the other
  • conservation of momentum: when the net external force is zero, the total momentum of the system is conserved or constant
  • constant: an unchanging value in an equation or describing a fundamental process in nature such as gravity
  • continuous spectrum: An uninterrupted distribution of colors according to wavelength
  • convection: movement caused within a gas or liquid by the tendency of hotter, and therefore less dense material, to rise and colder, denser material to sink under the influence of gravity, which consequently results in transfer of heat
  • density: the amount of mass per unit volume of an object
  • deposition: converting a gas to a solid
  • displacement: change in position of an object
  • distance: the magnitude or size of displacement between two positions
  • electromagnetic radiation: radiation consisting of waves propagated through regularly varying electric and magnetic fields and traveling at the speed of light
  • electromagnetic spectrum: the full range of electromagnetic waves, from radio to gamma rays
  • electron: a particle in an atom with a negative charge.
  • element: atoms that have the same number of protons, or the same atomic number.
  • emission spectrum: the spectrum that includes bright lines at the wavelengths related to the transition of an electron from a higher to lower energy level.
  • energy: a quantitative property that is able to do work or change a system
  • energy level: a unique energy value of possible electron orbits.
  • excitation: the transition to an excited state of an electron that has absorbed a specific amount of energy.
  • freezing point: the temperature where a liquid converts to a solid; can change with pressure
  • frequency: the number of waves that cross a given point per unit time
  • gamma rays: photons of electromagnetic radiation of energy with wavelengths no longer than 0.01 nanometer; the most energetic form of electromagnetic radiation
  • gravity: the mutual attraction of material bodies or particles
  • ground state: the lowest possible energy state of an electron in an atom.
  • heat: the transfer of energy from one object to another due to the difference in temperature between the two objects
  • infrared: electromagnetic radiation of wavelength 10^3–10^6 nanometers; longer than the longest red wavelengths that can be perceived by the eye, but shorter than radio wavelengths
  • ion: an atom that has gained or lost an electron, leaving it with a positive or negative charge instead of neutral
  • ionization: the removal or addition of one or more electrons from an atom
  • isotope: when an atom has a number of neutrons that is not equal to its atomic number, or number of protons
  • kinetic energy: the energy of an object due to its motion
  • mass: the amount of matter in an object
  • mass number: the total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus of an atom, A
  • mechanics: the study of the motion of objects in nature
  • melting point: the temperature that a solid converts to a liquid
  • metric system: a system of measurements that uses base units such as the meter and changes their scale by using decimal prefixes based on powers of 10
  • microwave: electromagnetic radiation of wavelengths from 1 millimeter to 1 meter; longer than infrared but shorter than radio waves
  • momentum: an object's momentum is its mass times its velocity
  • neutron: a particle in an atom with a neutral charge
  • Newton’s first law: every object will continue to be in a state of rest or move at a constant speed in a straight line unless it is compelled to change by an outside force
  • Newton’s second law: the change of motion of a body is proportional to and in the direction of the force acting on it
  • Newton’s third law: for every action there is an equal and opposite reaction or: the mutual actions of two bodies upon each other are always equal and act in opposite directions
  • nucleus: The center of an atom, consisting of protons and neutrons and having a positive charge.
  • opaque: when a material does not transmit light
  • periodic table: a table of the elements organized by atomic number, electron configuration, and other properties
  • phase diagram: a chart that depicts the dependence of the phase changes of a material on pressure and temperature
  • photon: a particle of electromagnetic radiation
  • plasma: a hot ionized gas
  • position: the location of an object in relation to a reference frame
  • potential energy: the energy of an object due to its position relative to a field, other objects, or the arrangement of particles inside it
  • pressure: force exerted per unit area
  • proton: a particle in an atom with a positive charge
  • quantum number: a number assigned to specific energy levels in an atom
  • radiation: emission of energy as electromagnetic waves or photons or the release of heat from an object
  • radio: the longest wavelength range of the electromagnetic spectrum
  • reflection: When light bounces off the surface of an object such as a mirror.
  • refraction: changing of the path of light when passing from one medium to another
  • scalar: a physical quantity that can be expressed by a single number such as speed
  • scientific notation: rewriting very large or very small numbers as a product of a decimal number and an exponent with base 10
  • spectral line: In a spectrum, light emitted or absorbed at a specific wavelength by an atom or molecule.
  • spectrum: The dispersion of light by wavelength.
  • speed: distance traveled divided by time during which motion occurs
  • Stefan-Boltzmann law: a formula from which the rate at which a blackbody radiates energy can be computed; the total rate of energy emission from a unit area of a blackbody is proportional to the fourth power of its absolute temperature: F = σT4
  • sublimation: converting a solid to a gas
  • temperature: the average translational kinetic energy of the molecules in an object or what is measured with a thermometer
  • time: the measurement of how long change occurs
  • transmission: When light passes through a material.
  • transparent: A material that light is able to pass through.
  • ultraviolet: electromagnetic radiation of wavelengths 10 to 400 nanometers; shorter than the shortest visible wavelengths
  • vaporization: the conversion of a liquid to a gas
  • variable: a part of an experiment or equation that can vary or change
  • vector: a quantity that includes both direction and magnitude where magnitude describes the size
  • visible: electromagnetic radiation with wavelengths of roughly 400–700 nanometers; visible to the human eye
  • volume: a measurement of the three-dimensional space that an object occupies
  • wavelength: the distance from crest to crest or trough to trough in a wave
  • weight: the force of gravity on an object; on Earth it is the mass of an object times the acceleration of gravity g
  • Wien's law: formula that relates the temperature of a blackbody to the wavelength at which it emits the greatest intensity of radiation
  • X-rays: electromagnetic radiation with wavelengths between 0.01 nanometer and 10 nanometers; intermediate between those of ultraviolet radiation and gamma rays
