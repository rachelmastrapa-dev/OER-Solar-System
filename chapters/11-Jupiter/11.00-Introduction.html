Introduction

We now leave the familiar realm of rocky planets as we venture past the asteroid belt to the outer planets. Unlike Venus, there is no solid surface below the clouds on any of the outer planets. This means that many of the properties that we take for granted on Earth, such as the length of a day, do not apply. These giants have more in common with the Sun than the Earth. We will first study Jupiter and its moons.

Learning Objectives
  • Describe the challanges of robotic exploration of the outer solar system
  • Describe the basic physical characteristics, general appearance, and rotation of the giant planets
  • Describe the discovery and characteristics of the giant planets' magnetic fields
  • Discuss the atmospheric composition of the giant planets
  • Understand the scale and longevity of storms on the giant planets
  • Describe the major features we can observe about Callisto and what we can deduce from them
  • Explain the evidence for tectonic and volcanic activity on Ganymede
  • Explain what may be responsible for the unusual features on the icy surface of Europa
  • Describe the major distinguishing characteristic of Io
  • Explain how tidal forces generate the geological activity we see on Europa and Io
