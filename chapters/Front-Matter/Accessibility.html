Other accessibility features were designed for improving screen reader functionality, including nested headers on each page, tables formatted with headers, limited use of bold or italics, and removal of long text strings such as URLs, with the exception of APA formatted references. Since astronomy is a visual science, a large part of this project has been ensuring that the textbook meets ADA and WCAG accessibility requirements. Figures have brief alternative text embedded within the image. For more complex figures, there is a link in the figure caption to an accessible description at the end of the chapter. These descriptions are a detailed visual description combined with an overview of the learning outcomes of the figure.

External resources that can't be changed by the authors are gathered in Further Exploration boxes. Some of them have been cleared during accessibility review. Those that may not work with assistive technologies are labeled.

Address Color and Visual Encoding Color is almost always important in astronomy figures: If there is no explanation of the meaning of color, ask the content expert Explicitly describe the meaning of color in text Always describe color in images For false color images, explain how the colors are assigned if available, or include the wavelength of the light. Example: An infrared image of the Crab nebula When describing the instructional takeaway, use color when it is relevant. Otherwise, describe what the color represents. This textbook is designed to make those resources as clear and accessible as possible for all learners. Instructional Visuals The book includes high-resolution, clearly labeled visuals, such as: Diagrams of anatomical planes, directional terms, and body cavities Micrographs and diagrams of cell structure and stages of cell division Representative tissue micrographs with key features identified Drawings and micrographs of skin layers and integumentary structures Images of bone classifications, bone tissue, including compact and spongy bone, and cartilage Large-scale illustrations of axial and appendicular skeletons with major bone markings Diagrams of joint types and body movements Micrographs and schematics of skeletal muscle tissue Gross anatomical images and diagrams of major muscle groups, including selected origins and insertions These visuals are chosen and designed to: Emphasize clarity and accuracy Support transfer-level rigor Help students connect structure to function and lab to lecture Whenever possible, the book draws on Creative Commons–licensed visuals and references. This facilitates local adaptation, customization for accessibility, and future updates by instructors and institutions. Accessibility Features The textbook reflects an equity-focused and accessibility-forward design philosophy. In both print and digital formats, it uses: Structured headings to support navigation and screen-reader use Readable typography and consistent page layout High color contrast for text and key visual elements Meaningful alternative text for figures and diagrams Descriptive hyperlink text instead of bare URLs Simple, tagged tables only when truly needed Captions and transcripts for media Plain-language explanations and instructions to reduce extraneous cognitive load These choices are intended to: Support students using screen readers or keyboard-only navigation Improve usability on different devices and platforms Align with Universal Design for Learning principles Help institutions meet accessibility and accreditation expectations By providing zero-cost course materials in accessible formats from the outset, the textbook aims to promote equitable access and support student persistence and success across diverse backgrounds and learning needs.
