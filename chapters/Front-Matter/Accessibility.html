Accessibility

This textbook was reviewed for compliance with WCAG and ADA requirements. The authors also took a course that included a review of accessibility guidelines, which included a broad range of reccomendations for the origanization and format of text. Since astronomy is a visual science, a large amount of time was recquired to ensure all figures were accessible. These methods benefit all students, including those with visual impairments, challenges with visual processing of information, or those that have less experience interpreting scientific schematics or graphs.

General Guidelines

Some accessibility requirements are met by the Libretexts platform where users can adjust the size of the text. While reading a textbook, a student can switch to reader mode which minimizes all menus to provide a more book-like experience with fewer distractions.

For students that use screen readers, the authors:

  • Included appropriate headers in tables.
  • Used appropriately formatted lists encoded in HTML by the editing UI.
  • Concise, descriptive hyperlink text.
  • Removed all text URLs except for those listed in APA citations.
  • Used short, descriptive heading titles with no special characters.
  • Organized pages under nesting headings for convenient navigation.
  • Limited use of italic and bold text.
  • Used capital letters for acronyms and did not use it for emphasis.

The accessibility reviewer read the textbook and also used the WAVE app.

Figures

All figures have alternative text embedded within the image and a figure caption. For more complex figures, there is a link in the figure caption to an accessible description at the end of the chapter. These descriptions are a detailed visual description combined with the key takeaways. Jyothsna Viswanadha provided a framework that we adapted with reccomendations that are specific to astronomy. These guidelines are based on these requirements:

  • WCAG 2.1 Success Criterion 1.1.1 (Text Alternatives) "All non‑text content must have a text alternative that provides equivalent meaning."
  • ADA Title II "Public institutions must provide equal access to instructional content for students with disabilities."

The alternative text requirements are:

  • Under 125 characters in length.
  • Describes the relevant object(s)
  • Reflects what a sighted student gains from the image.
  • Embedded directly with the image.
  • Does not start with “image of,” “picture of,” or “graphic of.”
  • Repeat caption text word‑for‑word.

Along with alternative text, each figure has a brief caption. The main purpose of the caption is to reflect the learning objective of the figure, not describe the figure in detail. For figures that require a detailed description, we either describe it in the text immediately before the figure or create an accessible description. The accessible descriptions are listed in a page at the end of the chapter and are linked to the caption.

An accessible description should include the major components of the figure, any relevant relationships or patterns, all labels or text, and the instructional takeaway. Always include diagram labels and map legends. For charts, include title, axis labels and ranges, and if the scale is linear or not. Describe the behavior of all data and values in the chart. In some cases creating a data table is better than a verbal description. Avoid describing every single detail in the image or content that is not present in the figure.

For figures in astronomy, color is often a very important characteristic. Include descriptions of any colors and what they represent. For false color images, explain how the colors are assigned if available, or include the wavelength of the light. Mention colors in key takeaways only if they are instructionally relevant.

Please see the AI Usage page to review how we used agentic AI to format accessible descriptions and other figure properties.

External Resources

Resources such as astronomy simulations and YouTube videos were reviewed for accessibility. For any simulations, the reviewer used Apple VoiceOver to simulate screen reader use, along with trying to interact using keyboard commands only. Many of these resources are in the Further Exploration boxes, and not part of the main instructional content. Some videos and simulations did not meet the accessibility requirements. However, since the Further Exploration boxes are not part of the main instructional content, and not required for the textbook, we may keep the link with an appropriate label. Any resources that are preceded by a statement similar to "This resource may not be accessible to assistive technologies" should not be part of any required materials.

Summary

We are excited to provide these accessible learning materials as OER so that they can be adapted and improved by educators and students. When making changes, please remember to keep these guidelines in mind. As technologies change, these guidelines may require updates. We hope to work with other educators to establish clear standards for everyone to follow.
