OER for Stellar and Galactic Astronomy

This textbook is part of the De Anza College Zero Textbook Cost (ZTC) Pathways initiative, to provide no-cost learning materials that are available on the first day of class. The material is designed for an introductory, general education astronomy class that has no math or science prerequisites, with a focus on stellar and galactic astronomy. At De Anza, this book is used for ASTR 10, a course that is CSU- and UC-transferable general education and satisfies CalGETC Group 5A — Physical Sciences.

Philosophy

The foundation of this textbook is Openstax Astronomy. The material was reorganized to align with a the course topic and structured to support the needs of a diverse population of undergraduate students. We understand that our students have a broad range of experience in math and science. For some of them, this is their first college-level science course, while others have already completed AP classes in math or science. For all students, the large amount of new scientific vocabulary can be a challlenge, especially if English is not their first language.

To address these challenges we have:

  • Simplified and streamlined the text for reduction of cognitive load and consistent translation, including reducing the use of idioms.
  • Included a list of key terms for every chapter that use student-friendly language.
  • Added material to Further Exploration boxes at the end of chapters for students to expand their knowledge.

These changes are designed top both lower barriers and encourage individual exploration for students with a range of academic preparation. For all students, we have used Creative Commons–licensed or public domain figures that they will always be able to access, while stressing the importance of confirming the source of images in the age of AI. A large amount of work went into making all content, especially the figures, compliant with ADA and WCAG guidelines. Please visit the page about accessibility for a detailed description.

Science and Society

To help students make a personal connection with astronomy, we included a page in each chapter discussing the interaction of astronomy and human society. These topics range from science in popular media to the naming of star to the ethics of building telescopes. We also includes stories of early astronomers from different cultures and how astronomy was part of their mythology and spiritual traditions. These sections are designed to encourage students to think about their own connection to the field of astronomy and can be used as the basis for class discussion. All resources for these articles are listed on the page, so that students can easily find more information.

To Be Continued

The power, and challenge, of OER is that it is never done. At the same time, it is much easier to change and update. Research in astronomy is moving at such a rapid pace that educaitonal materials need constant updating. As more accessible media, such as videos and simulations, become available, they can be integrated into this material. We encourage faculty to adapt and supplement this book, and ideally publish their own OER, which can then be adopted by other faculty. Together we can make high-quality, accessible, free textbooks for every student.
