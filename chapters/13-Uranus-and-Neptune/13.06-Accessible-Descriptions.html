Figure 13.1.1 Infrared Image of Uranus

Two false-color infrared Hubble Space Telescope images of Uranus, its rings, and moons, taken 90 minutes apart in 1997.

Left Panel

  • Uranus is a round disk in the center, colored green on the left, blue on the right, with an orange-red edge on the right side.
  • The green color shows a strong haze around the south pole, which faces the Sun.
  • A bright, curved light blue band runs from top to bottom across the left part of the disk.
  • Several small reddish clouds are near the right edge of the planet.
  • A bright gray ring forms a tall, narrow oval around the planet, so the planet sits in the middle like a bull's eye.
  • Small white dots scattered around the planet are moons.

Right Panel

  • The same view 90 minutes later, with labels.
  • The south pole is marked with a + in the green region on the left part of the disk.
  • A curved white line runs from top to bottom through the planet, marking the equator.
  • A short arrow just left of the equator points to a cloud feature.
  • The bright outer ring is the Epsilon Ring.
  • Eight moons each have a curved arrow showing how far the moon moved in 90 minutes. Starting at the top and moving counterclockwise, the moons are Belinda, Rosalind, Desdemona, Juliet, Cressida, Bianca, Portia, and Puck.

Takeaway

  • Uranus is tipped on its side, so one pole can point toward the Sun, and the rings and moons orbit around the equator in a pattern that looks like a target.

Figure 13.1.2 Seasons on Uranus

A diagram of Uranus at four positions in its orbit and a chart of the amount of sunlight reaching its equator and poles.

Diagram (a)

  • On the left, on a black background.
  • The Sun is a yellow circle in the center of an oval orbit, seen from above.
  • An arrow on the orbit shows that Uranus moves counterclockwise.
  • Uranus is a blue-green ball with a ring, shown at four positions. At each position, the axis of the planet is horizontal, with the south pole, S, on the left and the north pole, N, on the right.
  • Position 1, on the right, is the year 1986. The south pole faces the Sun.
  • Position 2, at the top, is the year 2007. The equator faces the Sun.
  • Position 3, on the left, is the year 2028. The north pole faces the Sun.
  • Position 4, at the bottom, is the year 2049. The equator faces the Sun.

Chart (b)

  • On the right.
  • Three panels are stacked on top of each other, labeled Equator, South pole, and North pole from top to bottom.
  • The vertical axis is labeled Amount of Sunlight, with no numbers.
  • The horizontal axis is labeled Earth Years and goes from 0 to 84 in increments of 21. The years match the positions in Diagram (a), with 0 at position 1.
  • Each curve is drawn in orange.
  • Equator: a wave that is highest at 0, lowest at 21, highest at 42, lowest at 63, and highest again at 84.
  • South pole: an arch that starts at zero at 0 years, peaks at 21 years, and drops to zero at 42 years. There is no curve from 42 to 84 years, when the south pole is dark.
  • North pole: no curve from 0 to 42 years, when the north pole is dark. Then an arch that starts at zero at 42 years, peaks at 63 years, and drops to zero at 84 years.

Takeaway

  • Because Uranus is tipped on its side, each pole has about 42 years of sunlight followed by 42 years of darkness during each 84-year orbit.

Figure 13.2.1 Neptune

A Voyager 2 image of Neptune, a deep blue sphere on a black background.

Image Elements

  • Neptune fills the center of the image, with the color exaggerated to a deep blue.
  • Faint, lighter blue bands run across the planet from left to right.
  • A large dark oval, the Great Dark Spot, is near the center, with bright white clouds along its lower edge.
  • A few small white clouds are scattered across the planet, including a small bright cloud at the lower left.
  • Near the bottom, a smaller dark spot and darker bands circle the area around the south pole.

Takeaway

  • Neptune has bands, clouds, and large storms similar to Jupiter. Its blue color comes from sunlight scattered in its upper atmosphere.

Figure 13.2.2 High Clouds on Neptune

A close-up Voyager image of thin, white cirrus clouds near the edge of Neptune.

Image Elements

  • The pale blue planet fills the left half of the image, and black space fills the right half.
  • The curved edge of the planet runs from top to bottom near the center of the image.
  • Long, thin white streaks of cloud run nearly from top to bottom, close to and parallel to the edge of the planet.
  • Thin dark lines beside the white streaks are shadows cast by the clouds on the cloud layer below.

Takeaway

  • These methane ice clouds look like cirrus clouds on Earth. Their shadows show they are about 75 kilometers above the main cloud layer.

Figure 13.3.1 Triton

A mosaic of Voyager 2 images of the southern part of Neptune's moon Triton.

Image Elements

  • The lower half of Triton's sphere curves across the bottom of the image, against black space.
  • Near the top, the surface is a smooth, pale gray-green, with fine cracks, ridges, and small pits.
  • An uneven border of bright white patches runs across the middle of the image.
  • The bottom half is the large southern polar cap, colored pinkish white.
  • Many dark streaks and spots are scattered across the polar cap, especially near the center and lower right.

Takeaway

  • Triton has a varied surface. The south polar cap faces the Sun, and slight heating drives some of its material north, where it is colder.

Figure 13.3.2 Triton's Geysers

A close-up, black and white Voyager 2 image of part of the polar cap of Triton, showing dark streaks from geysers.

Image Elements

  • The curved edge of Triton runs along the left side of the image, against black space.
  • The left part of the surface is bright and rough, with many short dark streaks that all point in the same direction, toward the lower right.
  • The center has light gray patches with irregular edges and darker blotches between them.
  • The right part of the surface is smoother and darker gray, with two long, parallel ridges near the upper right.

Takeaway

  • The dark streaks are long trains of dust from geysers of nitrogen gas, which form as the polar cap evaporates.

Figure 13.3.3 Rings of Uranus

A long exposure, black and white Voyager 2 image of the rings of Uranus.

Image Elements

  • Many thin, curved bands of light arc across the image from the lower left to the upper right. These are the rings.
  • The bands are brightest across the middle of the image.
  • Below the main bands, one thin, distinct ring is separated from the others by a dark gap.
  • The dark background has a grainy, speckled texture, which is noise from the camera electronics.
  • Short, bright streaks are scattered across the image, all tilted in the same direction.

Takeaway

  • The rings of Uranus are so narrow and dark that a long exposure was needed to see them.

Figure 13.3.4 Rings of Neptune

A long exposure, black and white Voyager 2 image of the rings of Neptune.

Image Elements

  • Neptune is at the upper left, so overexposed that it looks like a large white glow with a bright crescent inside.
  • Two thin, bright rings curve around the planet, from the top of the image down to the lower left.
  • The outer ring is brighter along part of its length, where the ring material is denser.
  • The dark background is speckled, with small white dots scattered across it.

Takeaway

  • Neptune's rings are thin and faint, and the outer ring has regions that are denser than the rest of the ring.

Figure 13.4.3 Trust in Science

A line graph tracking Americans' confidence in the scientific community from the 1970s to the 2020s, separated by political party.

Chart Elements

  • The vertical axis is labeled 'Great deal' of confidence in scientific community (%) and runs from 0 to 70 in increments of 10.
  • The horizontal axis shows years from 1970 to 2020.
  • A blue line labeled Democrats fluctuates between roughly 35% and 45% from the 1970s to the 2010s, then rises sharply to a peak of about 65% around 2020 before falling back to around 50% in the most recent years.
  • A red line labeled Republicans fluctuates between roughly 35% and 50% from the 1970s to the 2010s, then declines steadily after about 2015 to around 23% in the most recent years.
  • The lower right corner of the chart is labeled Current Opinion in Psychology, citing the chart's source.

Figure 13.4.4 Moon Joy

A photograph of the Artemis II crew sharing a moment of celebration aboard the Orion spacecraft.

Image Elements

  • Four crew members are gathered in a tight group hug, floating in microgravity inside the spacecraft.
  • American and Canadian flags are mounted on equipment above the crew.
  • Cargo nets holding blue and orange storage bags line the walls of the cabin.
