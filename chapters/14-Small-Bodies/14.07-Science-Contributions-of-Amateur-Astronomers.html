Science and Society - Contributions of Amateur Astronomers

Most textbooks have no shortage of discussion about the achievements of professional astronomers and other scientists. However, the field of amateur astronomy has been alive and well as a valid area for citizen scientists to contribute. That is, the topics covered in this section were not necessarily completed by someone with a degree or PhD in science.

The Solar System is estimated to have millions of objects, most of them being asteroids and comets, and many of which astronomers do not have the time or resources to track each one. Over the past several decades, amateur astronomers have discovered numerous comets as they gaze up at night with their personal telescopes. In addition, the Night Sky Network is a nationwide coalition of amateur astronomy clubs and organizations that promote science communication and networking among fellow citizen scientists.

Hale-Bopp Comet

Comet C/1995 O1 (Hale-Bopp) was discovered on July 23, 1995, independently, by amateur astronomers Alan Hale and Thomas Bopp. Hale-Bopp was discovered at the amazing distance of 7.15 AU.

Comets are usually named for their discoverer(s) or for the name of the observatory/telescope used in the discovery. Since both Alan Hale and Thomas Bopp discovered this comet it is named for them. The letter "C" indicates that C/1995 O1 (Hale-Bopp) is a long-period comet and is not expected to return to the inner Solar System. 1995 indicates the year of discovery.
