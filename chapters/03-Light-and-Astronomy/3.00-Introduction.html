Introduction

To study the planets, astronomers need to be able collect data. The main tool that most astronomers use is the telescope. This chapter will describe how telescopes work and the various instruments that astronomers use to collect data. It is understandable if the word instrument makes you think of a piano or a guitar. Scientific instruments are devices that are used by astronomers to collect data, including telescopes, detectors, and spectrometers. While objects in the Solar System are observed by some ground based telescope, a large amount of planetary data comes from spacecraft.

Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  • Describe the relationship between wavelength, frequency, and speed of light.
  • Discuss the particle model of light and the definition of photon.
  • List the regions of the electromagnetic spectrum.
  • Describe the structure of atoms and the components of nuclei.
  • Explain how astronomers learn the composition of a gas by examining its spectral lines.
  • Describe the main parts of a telescope and how it collects light.
  • List the different types of detectors and describe why the charge-coupled device (CCD) is preferred.
  • Review the difference between visible and infrared observations.
  • List the advantages of making astronomical observations from space.
  • Describe the different types of spacecraft used to explore the Solar System.
