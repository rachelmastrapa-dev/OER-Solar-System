Light and Astronomy Key Terms

  • absorption spectrum: the unique pattern of dark lines that form when light passes through a material and into a spectrometer
  • aperture: diameter of the primary lens or mirror of a telescope
  • charge-coupled device (CCD): array of high-sensitivity electronic detectors of electromagnetic radiation, used at the focus of a telescope or camera lens to record an image or spectrum
  • chromatic aberration: distortion that causes an image to appear fuzzy when each wavelength coming into a transparent material focuses at a different spot
  • continuous spectrum: an uninterrupted distribution of colors according to wavelength
  • electromagnetic radiation: radiation consisting of waves propagated through regularly varying electric and magnetic fields and traveling at the speed of light
  • electromagnetic spectrum: the full range of electromagnetic waves, from radio to gamma rays
  • electron: a particle in an atom with a negative charge
  • emission spectrum: the spectrum that includes bright lines at the wavelengths related to the transition of an electron from a higher to lower energy level
  • energy level: a unique energy value of possible electron orbits
  • excitation: the transition to an excited state of an electron that has absorbed a specific amount of energy
  • eyepiece: magnifying lens used to view the image produced by the objective lens or primary mirror of a telescope
  • filter: a type of window that absorbs certain colors of light while allowing others to pass through
  • flyby: a spacecraft mission that flies past its target object
  • focus: point in a telescope where the rays of light converged by a mirror or lens meet
  • frequency: the number of waves that cross a given point per unit time
  • gamma rays: photons of electromagnetic radiation with wavelengths no longer than 0.01 nanometer; the most energetic form of electromagnetic radiation
  • ground state: the lowest possible energy state of an electron in an atom
  • infrared: electromagnetic radiation of wavelength 103–106 nanometers; longer than the longest red wavelengths that can be perceived by the eye, but shorter than radio wavelengths
  • interference: process in which waves mix together such that their crests and troughs can alternately reinforce and cancel one another
  • interferometer: instrument that combines electromagnetic radiation from one or more telescopes to obtain a resolution equivalent to what would be obtained with a single telescope with a diameter equal to the baseline separating the individual separate telescopes
  • interferometer array: combination of multiple radio dishes to, in effect, work like a large number of two-dish interferometers
  • inverse square law: for light, the amount of energy flowing through a given area in a given time decreases in proportion to the square of the distance from the source of energy or light
  • lander: a spacecraft designed to set down on the surface of another celestial body
  • lens: an object, usually made of glass, that focuses or defocuses the light that passes through it
  • luminosity: the rate at which a star or other object emits electromagnetic energy into space; the total power output of an object
  • microwave: electromagnetic radiation of wavelengths from 1 millimeter to 1 meter; longer than infrared but shorter than radio waves
  • neutron: a particle in an atom with a neutral charge
  • nucleus: the center of an atom, consisting of protons and neutrons and having a positive charge
  • orbiter: an object that orbits another, especially a spacecraft that orbits a planet etc. without landing on it
  • photometer: an instrument used to measure the intensity of light
  • photon: a particle of electromagnetic radiation
  • prime focus: point in a telescope where the objective lens or primary mirror focuses the light
  • proton: a particle in an atom with a positive charge
  • quantum number: a number assigned to specific energy levels in an atom
  • radar: technique of transmitting radio waves to an object and then detecting the radiation that the object reflects back to the transmitter; used to measure the distance to, and motion of, a target object or to form images of it
  • radiation: emission of energy as electromagnetic waves or photons or the release of heat from an object
  • radio: the longest wavelength range of the electromagnetic spectrum
  • reflecting telescope: telescope in which the principal light collector is a concave mirror
  • reflection: when light bounces off the surface of an object such as a mirror
  • refracting telescope: telescope in which the principal light collector is a lens or system of lenses
  • refraction: changing of the path of light when passing from one medium to another
  • resolution: detail in an image; specifically, the smallest angular or linear features that can be distinguished
  • rover: a vehicle for exploring extraterrestrial bodies
  • spectral line: in a spectrum, light emitted or absorbed at a specific wavelength by an atom or molecule
  • spectrometer: an instrument for obtaining a spectrum; in astronomy, usually attached to a telescope to record the spectrum of a star, galaxy, or other astronomical object
  • spectroscopy: the study of the spectrum of an object to identify its properties
  • spectrum: the dispersion of light by wavelength
  • telescope: an instrument used to observe distant objects by collecting and focusing their light
  • ultraviolet: electromagnetic radiation of wavelengths 10 to 400 nanometers; shorter than the shortest visible wavelengths
  • visible: electromagnetic radiation with wavelengths of roughly 400–700 nanometers; visible to the human eye
  • wavelength: the distance from crest to crest or trough to trough in a wave
  • X-rays: electromagnetic radiation with wavelengths between 0.01 nanometer and 10 nanometers; intermediate between those of ultraviolet radiation and gamma rays
