# Terminology

Since students are most likely not familiar with astronomy, use this guide when selecting words in new text.

## General Terminology Guidelines

The textbook is for an introductory astronomy course with no math requirement. 

### Words to Use

- visible or visible light - referring to the wavelength range of light, not the viewability of an object.
- main-sequence - when referring to stars that are fusing hydrogen in their core.
- cross-section - general term in illustrations or diagrams that represent viewing an objects interior from the side, as if you could cut the object in half, or remove a slice.
- chart - use when referring to any kind of plotted data, including scatter, line, or bar. Use instead of plot, graph, etc.
- image - any data collected with a telescope or camera of a real object.
- diagram - a single or set of illustration that demonstrate the structure of an object, or explain the steps in a model, the design of a machine, etc.
- illustration - a representation of an object that is created by an artist. If it is generated by AI it must be labeled AI-generated.
- map - (noun) a visual representation of an object, landscape, or planet, that includes geographical elements such as latitude and longitude, cardinal directions, etc. It may or may not include data, such as a topographic map.
- H-R diagram - the chart of luminosity vs. temperature used to classify types of stars and understand their evolution.
- Solar System is a proper name and should always be capitalized. The general term for a star with planets is planetary system. 
- Objects orbit around each other. 
- The term galaxy is a general term and should be lower case, unless it part of a proper name. Examples: Andromeda Galaxy. There are five galaxies in this cluster.
- Libretexts is the name of the company that hosts the OER textbook.  Make sure that it is always capitalized and plural
- semi-major axis has a dash. replace semimajor with semi-major 
- For spacecraft, the official terms are crewed and uncrewed to clarify the presence of humans. For this textbook, the prefered terms are robotic or human spacecraft. If it says crewed or uncrewed, leave it alone. If it says manned or unmanned, change it to human or robotic, respectively.

### Words and terms to Avoid

- Revolve is a deprecated term for orbit and should not be used. Replace revolves about with orbits around
- Do not use the term optical to mean visible light.
- Avoid introducing new astronomy terms, such as diffraction spikes.
- Do not use italics
- Avoid sighted language
  - Use active language to locate objects. Example: The star is in the center of the image. Instead of: The star is visible in the image. or The star is shown in the image.
  - Avoid idioms involving sight meant for understanding. Example: Do you see how this works? Instead use: Do you understand how this works?
